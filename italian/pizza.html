Pizza

Description

The recipe described below is a complete recipe for an Authentic Italian Pizza (Margherita Style) and can be made easily with simple pantry staples. In a total of 2 hours (1.5 hours for dough rising, 15 mins prep, and 10 mins bake time), you can create a masterpiece in your own kitchen using just a standard oven and a baking tray.

While pizza has evolved into many styles globally, the traditional Neapolitan pizza is protected by tradition. It is defined by its thin, airy crust, San Marzano tomatoes, and fresh mozzarella. The secret lies not in complex toppings, but in the quality of the dough and the high heat of the "forno" (oven).

The process starts with a simple yeast-based dough that is allowed to ferment and rise. It is then hand-stretched—never rolled with a pin—to keep the air bubbles intact, topped with a vibrant tomato sauce, and baked quickly at the highest possible temperature to achieve a charred, crispy, yet chewy crust.

Ingredients

    • "00" Flour or All-purpose flour (High protein is best)
    • Warm Water
    • Active dry yeast
    • Salt
    • Sugar (a pinch to feed the yeast)
    • Tomato Sauce: Canned peeled tomatoes (crushed) or tomato purée
    • Cheese: Fresh Mozzarella (fior di latte) or Buffalo Mozzarella
    • Herbs: Fresh Basil leaves
    • Extra Virgin Olive Oil
    • Parmesan cheese (Optional, for a salty kick)

Complete Steps to make Italian Pizza

Preparation-The Dough

    The soul of Italian Pizza is its crust. This recipe makes two 10-inch pizzas.

  1. Activate Yeast: In a bowl, mix 3/4 cup warm water, 1 tsp yeast, and a pinch of sugar. Let it sit for 5–10 mins until foamy.
  2. Mix & Knead: Add 2 cups of flour and 1 tsp of salt to a large bowl. Pour in the yeast mixture. Mix until a shaggy dough forms.
  3. The Work: Move the dough to a floured surface. Knead for about 10 minutes until it is smooth, elastic, and bounces back when poked.
  4. The Rise: Lightly oil a bowl, place the dough inside, and cover with a damp cloth. Let it rest in a warm spot for 1 to 1.5 hours until it doubles in size.
  5. The Prep: Meanwhile, crush your canned tomatoes by hand or pulse in a blender with a pinch of salt. Slice your mozzarella into rounds or cubes and pat them dry with a paper towel to prevent a soggy pizza.

Shaping The Pizza

  1. Preheat: Turn your oven to its highest possible setting (usually 250°C to 275°C). If you have a pizza stone, put it in now. If not, use an inverted baking tray.
  2. Divide: Punch the air out of the risen dough and divide it into two balls.
  3. Stretch: On a floured surface, gently press the center of the ball with your fingers, pushing the air toward the edges to form a "cornicione" (crust). Gently stretch it into a circle. Do not use a rolling pin, as it will kill the air bubbles that make the crust light.

Make the Pizza

  1. The Base: Spread a thin layer of the tomato sauce over the dough, leaving an inch of the border bare.
  2. The Cheese: Distribute the mozzarella pieces evenly.
  3. The Finish: Drizzle a little extra virgin olive oil over the top and add 2–3 fresh basil leaves. (Some prefer adding basil after baking to keep it bright green).
  4. The Bake: Carefully slide the pizza onto the preheated stone or tray.
    • In a standard oven: Bake for 8–12 minutes until the crust is golden-brown and charred in spots, and the cheese is bubbling.
    • In a dedicated pizza oven: This takes only 60–90 seconds!

Serving

Remove the pizza from the oven. Let it sit for one minute so the cheese sets slightly. Drizzle one last drop of olive oil and sprinkle some grated Parmesan if you’re feeling indulgent.

Slice it up, hear that crunch of the crust, and enjoy the perfect balance of acid, fat, and carbs. You’ve just brought a taste of Naples into your home!
